## Essential supplies and water

**[R3] U.S. National Park Service.** [*Ten Essentials*](https://www.nps.gov/articles/10essentials.htm). Institutional outdoor guidance. Supports mandatory safety categories, including navigation, weather layers, illumination, first aid, repair/fire supplies, emergency shelter and extra supplies. It does **not** provide the example's gear masses. Route conditions and local regulations may add requirements.

**[R4] American Hiking Society.** [*The Ten Essentials of Hiking*](https://americanhiking.org/10essentials/). Supports a water starting guideline of about 0.5 L/hour in moderate conditions and carrying extra food. This is a planning guideline that needs adjustment. The extra 0.5 L reserve in our scenario is our assumption.

## Carrying allowance and personal characteristics

**[R5] Joe Pasteris, REI Expert Advice.** [*How Much Should Your Pack Weigh?*](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/backpacking-weight.html). Practitioner guidance gives approximately 10% of body mass for a loaded day-hiking pack and 20% for backpacking, with explicit exceptions. We use the day-hiking value only as a transparent planning heuristic. It is not a validated personal injury threshold or a rule that harder terrain permits higher loads.

**[R6] REI Expert Advice.** [*How to Size and Fit a Backpack*](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/backpacks-adjusting-fit.html). Explains torso-length and hip measurements for fitting. Supports measuring fit directly rather than deriving pack size from overall height. It does not supply a height-to-kilogram capacity formula.

**[R9] Gregory S. Walsh, Daniel C. Low and Marco Arkesteijn.** “Effect of stable and unstable load carriage on walking gait variability, dynamic stability and muscle activity of older adults.” *Journal of Biomechanics* 73 (2018), 18-23. DOI: [10.1016/j.jbiomech.2018.03.018](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2018.03.018). [Author manuscript and metadata](https://bura.brunel.ac.uk/handle/2438/19786). Fourteen older adults, mean age 65±6 years, completed short walking trials. Loading affected gait, with stability effects for unstable loads. This is context for age-related caution, not an age-to-capacity equation or a direct younger-versus-older comparison.

**[R10] Danielle M. Vickery-Howe et al.** “Physiological, Perceptual, and Biomechanical Responses to Load Carriage While Walking at Military-Relevant Speeds and Loads—Are There Differences between Males and Females?” *Biomechanics* 4(3) (2024), 382-410. DOI: [10.3390/biomechanics4030027](https://doi.org/10.3390/biomechanics4030027). [Publisher's full text](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-7078/4/3/27). A study of 23 soldiers found differences in relative physiological demand under the studied loads and speeds. Its military population and protocol limit direct application to recreational hiking. Biological sex comparisons do not establish an individual gender-based multiplier.

| Requested input | What the reviewed evidence supports | Treatment in milestone 1 |
|---|---|---|
| Body mass | General body-mass pack guideline [R5] | Assumed 70 kg adult and 7 kg planning cap |
| Age | Older-adult load-response evidence [R9] | No numerical age multiplier |
| Sex/gender | Population-specific biological sex findings [R10] | No blanket sex or gender coefficient |
| Height | Torso/hip fit measurements [R6] | No height-to-load formula |

**Research conclusion:** the sources reviewed here do not validate a universal equation `safe load = f(age, sex, mass, height)`. Absence of such an equation in these sources is not a proof that no related research exists. Individually assessed fitness, health, experience, pack fit, pace, terrain and conditions remain relevant. Future personalization needs additional validation for the intended population. For this project, keep the allowance as a stated external input.

## Explicit assumptions that need no invented citation

The 10 km distance, 500 m ascent, four-hour duration, mild weather, no refill, 70 kg body mass, inventory `(6,2)`, 630 kcal ration target, 33 g protein target, 0.5 L reserve, 25 L nominal pack if mentioned, fixed-kit masses, emergency reserve mass and packaging allowance are illustrative choices. The food targets define one modeled ration rather than total physiological needs. Volume is deferred. Equipment masses need measurement before any real scenario.
